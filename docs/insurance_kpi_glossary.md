# Insurance KPI Glossary

## Project Scope

This glossary defines the financial and operating metrics used to compare HDFC Life, SBI Life, ICICI Lombard, and Star Health. Historical analysis covers FY2022–FY2026, subject to data availability. All financial values will be recorded in ₹ crore unless otherwise stated.

## 1. Life Insurance Metrics

| Metric | Meaning | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Annualised Premium Equivalent (APE) | Annualised measure of regular and single premiums, using a consistently documented convention | Measures new business sales |
| Value of New Business (VNB) | Present value of expected future profits from new business, net of relevant costs | Measures value created by new policies |
| VNB Margin | VNB divided by APE, using the company's reported definition | Indicates new-business profitability |
| Embedded Value (EV) | Value of shareholders' interests in the existing insurance business under the reported methodology | Provides a basis for life insurer valuation |
| EV Growth | Year-on-year percentage change in embedded value | Tracks value creation |
| Persistency Ratio | Percentage of policies or premiums maintained after a specified period | Indicates customer retention |
| Solvency Ratio | Available solvency resources divided by required solvency margin, as reported | Measures capital adequacy |

## 2. General and Health Insurance Metrics

| Metric | Meaning | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Gross Direct Premium Income (GDPI) | Premiums from direct insurance business, as reported | Measures premium scale and growth |
| Gross Written Premium (GWP) | Gross premiums written during the period, following the company's reporting definition | Tracks business volume |
| Claims Ratio | Claims incurred divided by the premium denominator specified by the company | Measures claims experience |
| Expense Ratio | Relevant operating or acquisition expenses divided by the company's stated premium denominator | Tracks operating efficiency |
| Combined Ratio | Claims ratio plus expense ratio, when definitions are compatible | A ratio below 100% generally indicates underwriting profit before investment income |
| Underwriting Result | Underwriting income less claims and relevant expenses, using reported definitions | Measures insurance-operation profitability |
| Solvency Ratio | Available solvency resources divided by required solvency margin, as reported | Measures capital adequacy |

## 3. Financial Performance Metrics

| Metric | Meaning | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue / Income Growth | Year-on-year change in the selected reported income measure | Tracks business growth |
| Profit After Tax (PAT) | Reported profit after tax attributable to the relevant entity or shareholders | Measures accounting profitability |
| PAT Growth | Year-on-year percentage change in PAT; interpret carefully if prior-year PAT is zero or negative | Tracks earnings trajectory |
| Return on Equity (RoE) | Profit measure divided by average shareholders' equity, with the chosen definition documented | Measures profitability relative to equity |
| Book Value per Share | Relevant shareholders' equity divided by shares outstanding | Supports book-value analysis |
| Earnings per Share (EPS) | Reported earnings attributable to ordinary shareholders divided by weighted-average shares | Measures earnings per share |

## 4. Valuation Metrics

| Metric | Meaning | Typical Application |
|---|---|---|
| Price-to-Earnings (P/E) | Share price divided by earnings per share, or market capitalisation divided by comparable earnings | Profitable companies with meaningful earnings |
| Price-to-Book (P/B) | Market capitalisation divided by relevant shareholders' equity | Often useful for general insurers |
| Price-to-Embedded Value (P/EV) | Market capitalisation divided by comparable embedded value | Life insurers |
| Price-to-VNB (P/VNB) | Market capitalisation divided by annual VNB | Life insurers; interpret alongside growth and VNB margin |

## 5. Forecasting and Data Rules

1. Record the financial year, publication date, source URL, unit, and reported or calculated status for every observation.
2. Prefer annual reports, investor presentations, exchange filings, and regulatory disclosures.
3. Do not mix standalone and consolidated financial statements without explicitly identifying the choice.
4. Do not compare ratios unless their definitions and denominators are compatible.
5. Mark missing data as `NA`; do not invent or silently interpolate reported company metrics.
6. Clearly distinguish company-reported metrics from analyst-calculated metrics.
7. Document forecast assumptions separately from historical reported data.
8. Use consistent share-price dates and market-capitalisation dates when calculating valuation multiples.
9. Treat forecasts as analytical estimates, not company guidance unless explicitly identified as such.

## Disclaimer

This glossary supports an academic research project and is not investment advice. Metric definitions can differ across insurers and reporting periods; consult each company's disclosures before making comparisons.
